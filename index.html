07Debug Log

Issues While Building

Every system threw problems. Here are three that mattered — and how each was solved.

// ISSUE_01

Battery Size

PROBLEM

The hybrid's DC-DC converter only delivered ~80A — pull more than that and it drains over time.

FIX · CLASS-D + BASS REFLEX

Switched to a Class-D amp and cut larger holes in the woofer baffles.

// ISSUE_02

RCA vs Power Wires

PROBLEM

RCAs ran too close to power wires, causing a large buildup of noise through the speakers.

FIX · ISOLATED RUNS

Power wires routed through the right sills, RCAs through the left.

// ISSUE_03

Parasitic Draw

PROBLEM

Some converters and relays kept drawing power even with the car switched off.

FIX · KILL SWITCHES

Added five center-console switches to manually cut power to specific components.

FINGERPRINT
SYSTEM

An ESP32 and optical fingerprint sensor replace the ignition button — verifying driver identity before the car starts, and syncing DSP presets per user via Bluetooth.

COMPLETED · EARLY PROJECT
01How It Works

Driver auth at the ignition.

STEP 01

The driver places their finger on the optical sensor embedded in the ignition button surround.

STEP 02

The ESP32 compares the scan against stored templates — up to 127 registered users, false-acceptance rate under 0.001%.

STEP 03

On a match, a relay closes the ignition circuit. On failure the circuit stays open and an LED flashes red.

STEP 04

The authenticated user ID is broadcast over BLE to a paired phone, which loads that driver's DSP audio presets automatically.

02Tech Stack

Components & architecture.

⚡

ESP32

Dual-core MCU handling fingerprint matching, relay control, and BLE communication simultaneously without an RTOS.

◎

R307 Optical Sensor

250 dpi optical module with onboard flash for template storage — up to 127 enrolled fingers.

↯

12V Integration

A 12V-to-3.3V buck converter and automotive relay tie the system directly to the vehicle's battery and ignition line.

⇄

BLE 4.2

Broadcasts the authenticated user ID to a paired Android device for preset loading and remote-start unlock.

03Specs

Wired into the 12V system.

The ESP32 runs from a buck converter tapped at the fuse box. A 5A inline fuse protects the branch circuit.

The ignition relay is wired in series — if the ESP32 loses power, the relay defaults open so the car cannot start.

Arduino · FFT · LED Control

SOUND
LIGHTING

Audio from a speaker output jack is analyzed by an Arduino running a software FFT — individual LED banks react in real time to bass, low-mids, mids, and highs.

COMPLETED · EARLY PROJECT
01Signal Chain

From speaker jack to light.

STEP 01

Audio from a computer's speaker output is fed into the Arduino's analog input via a 3.5mm jack and a voltage divider.

STEP 02

The Arduino runs a 64-point software FFT to split the spectrum into four frequency bands.

STEP 03

Each band's running average is computed. Transient detection corrects for overall volume so the display stays reactive.

STEP 04

PWM-controlled transistors drive each LED strip independently — brightness tracks the energy of its band in real time.

02Frequency Bands

Four bands, four colors.

20 Hz – 300 Hz

Bass

OUTPUT · BLUE

Kicks and sub-bass produce a strong, sustained pulse. Highest energy band in most music.

300 Hz – 1 kHz

Low-Mids

OUTPUT · GREEN

Body of guitars, piano, and male vocals. Warm, sustained energy between beats.

1 kHz – 5 kHz

Mids

OUTPUT · AMBER

Presence and attack — snare cracks, vocal consonants, pick transients live here.

5 kHz – 20 kHz

Highs

OUTPUT · WHITE

Air and shimmer — cymbals, hi-hats, reverb tails and upper harmonics.

03Tech Stack

Hardware & software.

⚡

Arduino Uno

10-bit ADC samples audio at ~8 kHz. Handles FFT computation and four-channel PWM output in a tight main loop.

◎

Software FFT

64-point FFT bins mapped into the four target frequency ranges. No external DSP chip — all on the ATmega328P.

↯

Volume Normalization

Running average per band with transient peak detection prevents the display from flattening at low volumes.

⇄

LED Driver Stage

PWM-controlled NPN transistors drive each LED strip at up to 1A. No LED controller IC needed.
