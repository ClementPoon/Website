DOOM-Inspired Arcade Machine

Course project · Intro to Embedded Systems

Best project, in-class competition Demo video ↗

A first-person shooter written from scratch in C++ for a 32-bit microcontroller, built into a 3D-printed arcade cabinet.

Key specifications
MCUTI MSPM0G3507, Arm Cortex-M0+ at 80 MHz
GraphicsRaycasting 3D renderer with depth-sorted animated sprites on an ST7735 TFT (SPI)
AudioDouble-buffered streaming from an SD card to a 12-bit DAC, driving a separate amplifier
EnclosureSelf-designed 3D-printed cabinet with a speaker enclosure, powered by a 5 V external battery

What I did

  • Wrote the rendering system: a raycaster plus depth-sorted, animated sprites.
  • Built the audio engine: one buffer fills from the SD card while the other plays, getting enough throughput out of the 80 MHz clock for glitch-free music, footsteps, and effects.
  • Integrated the TFT and SD-card asset loading over SPI, and designed and printed the cabinet.
4.2

Payload Power & Sensor Board

Longhorn Rocketry Association · Payload subteam · Aug 2025 – present

  • PCB design
  • LiFePO₄ + BMS
  • Buck converter
  • MOSFETs
  • I²C / SPI
  • Python

Power and sensing hardware for the rocket's payload: a custom board that keeps the payload's Raspberry Pi on regulated 5 V while switching high-current actuators straight off the battery.

Key specifications
SourceFour LiFePO₄ cells (4S, 12.8 V nominal) with a BMS
RegulationBuck converter supplying regulated 5 V to a Raspberry Pi 4
Load switchingNitinol actuators powered from the battery through on-board MOSFETs
SensingBerryIMU and thermocouple over I²C/SPI, logged in Python

What I did

  • Designed the power schematic and PCB: battery pack, BMS, and buck converter for stable 5 V through flight.
  • Routed actuator power through MOSFETs on the board so the Pi's GPIO only sends a low-current control signal.
  • Interfaced the IMU and thermocouple and wrote the Python logger that records motion, altitude, and temperature from launch through landing and recovery.
4.3

Vehicle Power, Electrical & Audio System

Personal build · my Toyota Corolla Hybrid · ongoing

  • 12 V distribution
  • Relays
  • Buck converters
  • ESP32
  • BLE
  • Sensors
  • Harnessing

A long-running build on my own car: a 12-speaker audio system, a dashboard model car that mirrors the real car's lights, and the 12 V power and protection to run it all.

Key specifications
Audio12 speakers on 3 amplifiers, with a DSP in the center console
Power4 AWG feed through the firewall to distribution blocks; peaks near 70 A (≈900 W)
Control8 relays, buck converters (12 V → 5 V) for the microcontrollers, and a BLE link from the trunk amp rack to a front-mounted controller that drives the displays
ProtectionTemperature, humidity, vibration, and sound sensors; automatic power-off on overheating, high humidity, or sudden movement
StatusOngoing: rebuilding the ESP32 relay controller after a board failure

What I did

  • Planned the power distribution and wired the system myself, from the 4 AWG battery feed to the relay boards.
  • Wired a dashboard model car to mirror the real car's turn signals, headlights, and brake lights in real time through 8 relays.
  • Tracked down and fixed battery drain, speaker noise, and parasitic draw (below).
Engineering notes: problems & fixes
Problems found during the build
SymptomRoot causeFix
Battery drained over timeThe hybrid's DC-DC converter supplies only ~80 A to the 12 V battery, and the audio system peaks near 70 A, leaving little headroomSwitched to a more efficient Class D amplifier and opened up the woofer baffles to use the full door cavity
Noise through the speakersRCA signal cables ran too close to the power wiresPower runs down the right door sills and RCAs down the left, isolating the two
Parasitic draw with the car offDC-DC converters and relays drew power continuouslyAdded 5 center-console switches to cut power to individual components
